## IMPPA asks PM Modi’s help in lifting ‘Bengal Files’ ban  

### Synopsis  
The Indian Motion Picture Producers’ Association (IMPPA) has written to Prime Minister Narendra Modi over the ongoing controversy surrounding *The Bengal Files*. The film was released on **5 September 2025** but is reportedly facing an unofficial ban in **West Bengal**, with **most theatres refusing to screen it**. Although certified by the **Central Board of Film Certification (CBFC)** and legally permitted for public exhibition, the film is not being shown due to a **collective decision by theatre owners**.  

## Producers, distributors facing intimidation: IMPPA  

### Film’s plight  
IMPPA’s letter was addressed to **Prime Minister Narendra Modi** and stated, “**Though not officially banned**, the film has been subjected to indirect restrictions that have denied the public its rightful opportunity to view it.” The association said producers and distributors are **facing intimidation and other obstacles** (without claiming any government subsidies) and did not single out any particular authority as the source of the intimidation. They alleged that **the state government** had failed to provide a safe environment for the film’s release.  

## FWICE also raised its voice against the ban  

### Ongoing debates  
The controversy surrounding *The Bengal Files* has sparked intense debate about censorship, politics, and artistic rights. The film has been described as a **serious, controversial work dealing with sensitive historical events**, rather than a light‑hearted comedy. It delves into sensitive topics such as the **1946 Calcutta riots**, which adds to its controversial nature. The **Federation of Western India Cine Employees (FWICE)** condemned the ban, calling it “**shocking** that a CBFC‑certified film faces such restrictions.”  

## Film’s portrayal of Gopal Patha draws family criticism  

### Family’s disappointment  
The film’s depiction of **Gopal Patha** (also referred to in some sources as Gopal Mukherjee), a key figure purportedly protecting Hindus during the 1946 riots, has attracted criticism. **His grandson expressed disappointment** over the portrayal and **filed a complaint against director Vivek Agnihotri** (rather than sending a letter of appreciation to producer Karan Johar). A formal complaint has been recorded; the family has not denied taking legal action.
